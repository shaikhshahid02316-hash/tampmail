What is Temporary Email? (2026 Definition)

Temporary email (also known as temp mail, disposable email, or burner email) is an email address designed for short‑term use. It requires no registration, collects zero personal data, and automatically self‑destructs after a set period—typically 24 hours. Unlike permanent addresses, temporary email acts as a privacy shield, intercepting unwanted messages and preventing AI‑powered tracking systems from linking your identity across different services.

How Temporary Email Works Technically

Behind the simplicity, temporary email services employ robust infrastructure:

  • Random Generation: Cryptographically secure algorithms generate unpredictable addresses (e.g., a7f3k@tampmail.xyz).
  • SMTP Reception: Incoming emails are accepted via standard SMTP servers, then stored in volatile memory (RAM) only—never written to disk.
  • Unique Inbox URL: Each address gets a private, unguessable URL for access; no login or password is required.
  • Auto‑Deletion: After the TTL (time‑to‑live), cryptographic erasure overwrites the memory, leaving no forensic trace.
